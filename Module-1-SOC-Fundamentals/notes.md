## 📖 1. SOC Fundamentals & Architecture

### What is a Security Operations Center (SOC)?
A Security Operations Center (SOC) is a centralized command center within an organization. It is not just a room full of monitors, but a combination of **People, Processes, and Technology** working 24/7/365 to detect, analyze, and respond to cybersecurity incidents. The ultimate goal of a SOC is to minimize the impact of a breach by reducing the time it takes to detect and respond.

### Core Functions of a SOC:
1.  **Continuous Monitoring:** Constantly watching network traffic, endpoint activities, and logs to identify anomalies.
2.  **Alert Triage:** Analyzing alerts generated by security tools (like SIEM or EDR) to determine if they are true threats or false positives.
3.  **Incident Response (IR):** Following a structured methodology (NIST or SANS) to contain, eradicate, and recover from an active breach.
4.  **Threat Intelligence (TI):** Consuming and acting upon information about current and emerging threats (e.g., known malicious IPs, new malware strains).
5.  **Threat Hunting:** Proactively searching for attackers who have bypassed automated defenses.
6.  **Compliance & Auditing:** Ensuring the organization meets regulatory requirements (e.g., GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS).

### SOC Tiers & Roles (The Tiered Model):
Most modern SOCs operate on a tiered structure to efficiently manage alert volumes and prevent analyst burnout:
*   **Tier 1 (Alert Analyst / Triage):** The first line of defense. They monitor the SIEM dashboards, perform initial triage, filter out false positives (FPs), and escalate legitimate incidents to Tier 2. They rely heavily on Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
*   **Tier 2 (Incident Responder):** Takes escalated incidents. They perform deep-dive investigations, analyze endpoint and network artifacts, determine the scope of the breach, and recommend remediation steps.
*   **Tier 3 (Threat Hunter / SME):** Proactive defenders. They don't wait for alerts; they actively hunt for hidden threats in the network. They also perform advanced malware reverse engineering and create new detection rules (Sigma, YARA).
*   **SOC Manager / Director:** Handles the administrative side—staffing, training, reporting to executives (CISO), and defining SOC metrics.

### Key SOC Metrics:
*   **MTTD (Mean Time to Detect):** How long it takes to discover a threat. Lower is better.
*   **MTTR (Mean Time to Respond):** How long it takes to contain and resolve a threat after detection.
*   **Alert Volume & False Positive Rate:** Monitoring the noise level to prevent "alert fatigue" among analysts. A high FP rate means the SOC needs better tuning.

## 🛠️ 2. Home Lab Setup Plan & Architecture

To practice the skills of a SOC analyst safely, we must build an isolated environment. Working directly on your host machine with malware or attack tools is extremely dangerous and can compromise your personal data.

### Hardware & Software Requirements:
*   **Host Machine:** Minimum 16GB RAM (32GB recommended for running multiple VMs simultaneously), 100GB+ free SSD storage.
*   **Hypervisor (Virtualization Software):** Oracle VirtualBox (Free & Open Source) or VMware Workstation Player. I will be using VirtualBox for this lab.

### Virtual Machines (VMs) to be Created:
1.  **Windows 10/11 (Victim/Target):** This will simulate a corporate employee's workstation. We will install Sysmon (System Monitor) and Splunk Universal Forwarder on it later to generate and forward logs.
2.  **Kali Linux (Attacker):** The offensive security distribution. We will use this to launch simulated attacks (Nmap scans, brute force) against the Windows VM to generate malicious logs.
3.  **Ubuntu Server (SIEM/Log Server):** This will eventually host the Splunk Enterprise server or Elastic Stack to ingest, parse, and analyze the logs from the Windows VM.

### Network Configuration (Crucial for Safety):
*   **Isolated Network:** All VMs must be configured on a **"Host-Only"** or **"Internal Network"** adapter. This ensures that any malware executed or attacks launched stay within the virtual environment and do not touch the actual internet or your home network.
*   **Static IP Addressing:** Assigning static IP addresses to the VMs (e.g., Windows: 192.168.56.10, Kali: 192.168.56.20) makes it easier to set up log forwarding and SIEM configurations in later modules.

## ⚠️ Important Lab Safety Rules
1.  **Never bridge the malware analysis VM to your physical network.** Always use Host-Only.
2.  **Take Snapshots:** Before installing any new tool or running any suspicious file, take a snapshot of the VM. If it breaks, you can revert instantly.
3.  **Keep Host OS Updated:** Ensure your physical computer's antivirus and OS are up to date as a last line of defense.
